Terminology

Dispreferred term: "outcome." Avoid "outcome" in new writing for this project. Apply this preference going forward. The production codebase still uses "outcome" as its umbrella term — see § Codebase outcome codes below for the one-to-one mapping to editorial sector codes.

Decomposition terms

A brief never reports a difference as a single number. Every difference from today is split into two parts, and the two are always reported separately. The canonical names for those two parts are climate effect and operations effect (Eric, 2026-08-03).

TermWhat it measures
climate effectThe change in the current-operations value from its value under the historical hydroclimate. The same for every strategy at a given place and hydroclimate, so it is reported once per hydroclimate rather than once per cell.
operations effectA strategy's value minus the current-operations value under the same hydroclimate. Reported per strategy and per hydroclimate.

Together with the anchor: today + climate effect + operations effect = scenario, where today is current operations under the historical hydroclimate (s0020). Reporting the net difference without the split is not sufficient — the two terms routinely oppose each other, so a scenario that has moved a long way under both can read as though nothing happened.

Retired alternative: "hydroclimate contribution" and "strategy contribution." These name the same two quantities and carry the same arithmetic. They are retired as of 2026-08-03. Do not introduce them in new writing.

The codebase caught up on 2026-08-03, the same day the paragraph this replaces was written. The builder and all 23 published results briefs, the baselines brief and the place figure emit the canonical pair; machine identifiers were left untouched and no number moved.

What a tier is, and what may be read from it

Recorded 2026-08-05b from Eric, who assigned the scale. It had never been written down, and it is the rationale underneath every threshold decision the project has made.

A tier is entirely a COEQWAL construction. Nothing in CalSim 3 generates or calculates one. The model produces complex hydrologic output; a tier is a summary built on top of it.

And it is built by people. The tier values in each sector are produced by the project's subject area experts for that sector, working from the per-sector basis in the table above. The tier scale is therefore expert judgment expressed on a common scale, not a formula applied to model output — which is why the resolution question below has no external answer and why no one outside the project can supply one.

Its purpose is comparison across every modeled use, in both directions. The nine sectors are measured in units that have nothing to do with each other — revenue impact, delivery frequency, flow against a functional target, salinity exceedance, storage percentile, population abundance — and none of them can be compared with another in its native form. The tier scale exists so that any modeled use can be held beside any other, and so that each can be compared across scenarios. Agriculture beside community water systems is one pair of thirty-six; the scale is not built for that pair or any other in particular.

Because a tier is already a heavy summary, its continuous value does not carry fine structure that can safely be interpreted. Two area values differing in the second decimal are not thereby different in any way a reader should act on.

The continuous value is constructed differently in each sector

Recorded 2026-08-07 from a reading of all nine sector method drafts. It had been carried nowhere.

Every sector reports a continuous value banded the same way: 1.00–1.99 within Tier 1, and so on through 4.00–4.99. That banding is shared across all nine. The construction behind it is not. Five different constructions are in use:

constructionsectors
position within the tier's own intervalreservoir storage, Delta exports, winter-run salmon, agricultural revenue
a summary of a score distribution, μ − 0.25·SDDelta estuary ecology
ratios against a reference slope, explicitly non-linear within a tiergroundwater storage
a count of qualifying years, taking only six or eleven discrete values and never exceeding x.91environmental flows
the worse of two rescaled criteria; the average of threecommunity water systems; freshwater for in-Delta uses

Two consequences follow. Where the value is a position within the interval, the whole-number part equals the discrete tier by construction; where it is not, that is not guaranteed. And in three sectors — agricultural revenue, winter-run salmon, Delta exports — Tier 1 is defined as open-ended, so the interpolation has no upper anchor. Delta exports anchors to the highest value observed among Tier 1 scenarios in the analyzed set, which makes those decimals depend on the composition of the set rather than on a fixed scale.

No document may state or imply that a continuous value means the same thing across sectors. A continuous value is read against its own sector's construction. This sits alongside the 0.1 threshold below: that rule limits how finely a tier may be read, and this one limits across what it may be compared.

The 0.1 threshold, and what it is not

MOVEMENT = 0.10 is an interpretation threshold, assigned rather than derived. Eric set it. There is no way to define the true threshold quantitatively, and it may not exist at all — the question "how large a tier difference is meaningful" has no measurable answer, because a tier is a designed instrument rather than a measured quantity.

Three things follow, and the third is the one that keeps being got wrong:

  1. It equals one printed display step, which is deliberate: the project does not assert a difference finer than it shows.
  2. It applies at every grain — figure, prose, counts, tables.
  3. A difference below it is not noise, and no COEQWAL document may call it that. It is unstated. The project has established nothing about whether such a difference is real; it has decided not to speak about it. "Dead zone", the original name for this constant, invited the wrong reading and is retired.

If someone asks whether a 0.08 difference is real, the truthful answer is: we have not established that, and the briefs are built not to say.

And there is no one to ask. Closed 2026-08-05b: the question of putting a meaningful-difference threshold to the data team is a category error, because CalSim 3 does not produce tiers and the data team does not assign them. The sector experts who do assign them are working at the resolution of the tier bands themselves, not below. There is no external authority on the resolution of a scale the project built. The threshold is a judgment about how finely a summary may be read, it is Eric's to set, and it is set at 0.1.

Public-facing tier labels are Optimal / Acceptable / At-risk / Critical (per AC Workshop intro deck, slide 15). Earlier draft copy used "Suboptimal" for tier 2 — that has been retired. Groundwater storage carried a sector-specific tier-2 label of "Compromised" until 2026-07-29, when it was normalized to Acceptable: the four labels are uniform across all nine sectors, and no sector holds a label of its own.

The thresholds behind the labels remain sector-specific — that is the point of concept 11, tiers are calibrated within sectors, not across them. Groundwater storage reaches tier 2 when the storage trend is stable or increasing while average total storage sits below historical. That condition is what the label "Compromised" was reaching for, and it is preserved in the tier definitions in COEQWAL_project_description.md and coeqwal_website_copy.md. What is removed is the distinct label, not the distinct threshold.

Brief kinds

Two tiers, both public labels. Named by R19, _meta/brief_structure_rulings.md, 2026-08-04.

Results briefs report modeled tier results. Four kinds:

kindsubjectinternal key
strategy resultsone management strategy across five hydroclimates—
baseline resultsthe baseline scenario across five hydroclimates—
place resultsone placeLOI
sector resultsone area of the water system across every strategy—

Reference briefs describe what a reader needs in order to read a results brief: the strategy briefs, the hydroclimates brief, the Level 1 theme briefs. They carry no tier data.

Tier framework briefs are a reference brief kind: one per sector, describing how that sector's indicator is computed from model output and how its values map onto the tier scale. Nine prototypes exist as of 2026-08-07 in the briefs repository, generated by build_tier_framework_briefs.py. Their authorship is unsettled and with project leadership. They were drafted from method documents authored by the sector teams, who have objected to the rewriting; by word count 58–72% of each brief is newly written prose. Do not treat them as a settled document class until that closes. Account: _meta/sessions/session_close_2026-08-07_tier_framework_briefs.md; measurement: COEQWAL_brief_provenance_2026-08-07.html.

The pairing that motivated the split: a strategy brief says what a strategy is, and a strategy results brief says what it produces. The two are paired one-to-one by slug.

Theme brief naming

Level 1 theme briefs use sentence case in both inline prose and Related-briefs lists. The two canonical full forms most prone to inconsistency:

  • "Farms and groundwater" (not "Farms and Groundwater")
  • "Rivers, salmon and the Delta ecosystem" (not "Rivers and Salmon and the Delta Ecosystem", and not "Rivers and Salmon and Delta Ecosystem")

The other Level 1 names ("Community water systems", "The Delta as a living place") follow the same sentence-case rule.

Baseline results is not a theme brief and does not follow this section's rules. It is one of the four results briefs (see Brief kinds above), and it takes sentence case in prose like every other brief name. The all-caps display form it carried until 2026-08-04 is retired.

That form existed because the brief was filed in this section, among theme briefs, while the rule itself recorded that it was distinct from them. An exception was doing the work a category should have done. R19 supplies the category.

Card-short forms. In tight horizontal display contexts like the Findings tab card grid, theme brief titles may use a shorter display form. The canonical card-short forms are:

  • "Farms and groundwater" (identical to the full form)
  • "Rivers and salmon" (short form for ECO; the full form "Rivers, salmon and the Delta ecosystem" is used inline and in Related-briefs lists)

The other two Level 1 themes fit the available card width without truncation and have no separate short form. Where present, the card teaser paragraph carries the longer framing that the short title doesn't.
