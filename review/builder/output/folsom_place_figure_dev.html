Folsom Reservoir — reservoir storage: climate and operations effects, with the sector mean (dev preview, not published)

The 23 strategies are shown in three stacked panels that share one tier axis. The split is positional and carries no meaning; the panels are read as one figure continued.

The vertical axis is the continuous tier value from 1 to 4.99, Tier 1 at the top, with the tier bands as shaded zones. Each strategy occupies a column of five lanes, one per hydroclimate, in the order Historical, Moderate, Moderate-high, High, Extreme climate stress. The warm arrow is the climate effect: it runs from today (current operations under the historical hydroclimate) to current operations under each hydroclimate. It is drawn once, in the leading column, because it is the same for every strategy at this location. In each strategy lane the blue arrow is the operations effect, measured from the dashed guide — current operations under that same hydroclimate — to this strategy's value, so its length is how far the value shifts under the strategy. The solid level is this location's scenario value. A flat tick in place of an arrow means a shift smaller than the 0.10 movement threshold. The hollow diamond is the sector-mean marker: the unweighted mean of the reservoir storage value over the area's locations under the same strategy and hydroclimate, an average that includes Folsom itself. Every level and marker is listed in the table below, floored to one decimal.

How to read this page

Every strategy in the registry is reported here, under every hydroclimate it is served in. Nothing is chosen for being large, small or interesting, and no result is drawn or printed with more emphasis than another. One strategy is absent — 4.2 Prioritizing functional delivery levels to community water systems — because its five runs are not present in the 2026-08-01 snapshot this page reads, so it cannot be decomposed and is omitted rather than imputed.

Tier values are reported to one decimal place throughout — in the prose, in the figure and in the table. The tier scale does not carry meaning finer than that, and printing three decimals would imply a precision the modeling does not support. Each contribution shown is the difference between the floored values, so the terms add exactly as printed.

The difference between any cell and today separates into the part contributed by the hydroclimate and the part contributed by the management strategy. The climate effect is measured on current operations under the same hydroclimate, so it does not depend on which strategy is being read: it is constant down a column, which is why it is drawn once in the figure and printed once in the column header, though it is repeated in each cell so a row reads on its own. The operations effect is the distance along the tier axis from current operations under the same hydroclimate, never from the historical baseline. Where the two run in opposite directions the difference from today is smaller than either term alone, and those cells are shaded.

Each cell carries a continuous tier value and the tier band it falls in. The band is the floor of the modeled value. The four bands are definitional categories supplied by the tier framework — Tier 1 Optimal, Tier 2 Acceptable, Tier 3 At-risk, Tier 4 Critical — and the framework supplies the names, while what a band means for any reader, community or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

Each cell of the table below reads top to bottom as five lines. The first is the climate effect, the shift from today to current operations under that hydroclimate. The second is the operations effect, the shift from current operations under that hydroclimate to this strategy. The third is this location's tier value under the strategy, tagged with its tier band; the fourth spells that band out. The fifth is the sector-mean line: this location's value minus the unweighted mean over the reservoir storage area's locations, a mean that includes Folsom itself. A negative sector-mean line places this location lower on the tier axis — nearer Tier 1 — than the average for its area, and a positive one places it higher; the sign is a position on the axis and nothing more.

What this doesn’t say

This brief reports modeled outcomes from a specific ensemble. It does not predict what will happen, evaluate whether outcomes are acceptable, or recommend any course of action. The tier framework provides definitional categories; the meaning of those categories for any individual reader, community, or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

A single location read on its own says nothing about the others in its area. The sector mean shown here is one summary of that area; it does not describe any other individual location in it.

Where the numbers come from

Every value on this page is computed from the 2026-08-01 snapshot through source.py and decompose.py — the same modules the 23 results briefs and the baselines brief read — not transcribed from a results brief and not read from a sidecar.
