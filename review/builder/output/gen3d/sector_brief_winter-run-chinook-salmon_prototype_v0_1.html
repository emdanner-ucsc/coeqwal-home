What this is

This is a results brief: it reports the modeled tier results, and nothing else, for one area of the water system — winter-run Chinook salmon — under every management strategy in the ensemble, each simulated once under each of five hydroclimates. It transposes the grid the strategy results briefs report: one area, every strategy, rather than one strategy, every area. The selection rule is that there is none: this brief reports every management strategy these model runs carry a tier for, across all five modeled hydroclimates, and no filter is applied to either. 22 of the 23 management strategies are reported under every hydroclimate. The Delta Conveyance Project runs report no winter-run Chinook salmon tier, and that strategy is shown as such. A scenario is a strategy paired with one hydroclimate; the five hydroclimates are a sample of stress levels, not a forecast of which will occur. For how the five were constructed, see the hydroclimates reference brief.

What the model shows

Across the 22 strategies these runs report, the modeled tier value under today — current operations under the historical hydroclimate — is 4.2, the same value for every row. Read across the five hydroclimates, the values this brief reports range from 3.4 to 4.9. Of the 110 results — one strategy under one hydroclimate — 24 sit in a different tier band from today and 86 sit in the same band; in 18 of the 22 strategies the five values do not all sit in one band.

The figure below draws every one of those results beside today, with the two effects that produce the difference shown as separate marks, so a reader can see how much of each difference the hydroclimate carries and how much the strategy does. The section that follows says what the two effects are before the figure draws them; what the tier values themselves mean is set out in how to read the values.

Today, and the two effects

Today is current operations under the historical hydroclimate. It is the one reference this brief asks a reader to learn, and it is the same value in every column, because it does not depend on which hydroclimate a scenario pairs with.

Any difference between a scenario and today comes from two places, and they add exactly:

The climate effect is current operations under that hydroclimate minus current operations today — the same operations, a different hydroclimate. The operations effect is that strategy minus current operations under that same hydroclimate — the only comparison that holds the hydroclimate fixed, and therefore the only one that isolates what the strategy does. Together they equal the difference from today.

Tier results are reported as a value on a continuous axis from Tier 1 to Tier 4.99, with the four tier bands as shaded zones; the whole number below any value is its band. A value that lands exactly on a whole number takes that band: 4.0 is Tier 4, at the start of its zone rather than the top of Tier 3. The tier scale is ordered by definition, Tier 1 through Tier 4; a band is a category and a position on the axis is a modeled magnitude, neither is a judgment. The scale, the two thresholds and the two fields a tier value comes in are set out in how to read the values.

Winter-run Chinook salmon’s tier value is the value at its one location, and every row on this page is measured at that same location: the rows differ in which strategy produced the value, never in what was measured. No averaging takes place, because there is one location to average. This page reports areas, not individual locations; where a place brief has been published for one of an area’s locations, that brief reports the location’s own values across the same scenarios.

How many places each strategy reaches

A strategy’s rules apply in some places and not others, and this area’s value is the unweighted mean of its locations, so a real change at a few of them reads as a small area-level difference. A location is counted where its value differs from current operations by at least 0.1 tier units. Of the one location behind this area, the number a strategy changes runs from 0 — Current operations, at the hydroclimate where it reaches fewest — to 1, at Current USBR operations where it reaches most. Four of them differ at no location under any hydroclimate. The per-strategy counts, split by hydroclimate and by direction, and the median difference behind each, are in the companion data file at #/rows/*/reach.

Read across the five hydroclimates together, 58 of the 110 location-and-hydroclimate results reported here differ from current operations: 44 toward Tier 1, 14 toward Tier 4. The median difference over the locations counted runs from 0.1 tier units at Relax Delta salinity standards to 0.3 at Salmon-friendly flows. What this doesn’t say

This brief reports modeled results from a specific ensemble. It does not predict what will happen, evaluate whether those results are acceptable, or recommend any course of action. The tier framework provides definitional categories; the meaning of those categories for any individual reader, community, or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

In particular, for this brief: the five hydroclimates are a sample of stress levels, carry no probabilities, and are not a timeline, so no average over them is reported here. Splitting a difference into two effects is arithmetic on modeled values, not a causal claim about the water system, and the two are separable only because each is defined against its own reference. A step or a spread on the tier axis is a modeled magnitude, not a benefit or a cost. An area’s value is the unweighted mean of its locations, so locations count equally regardless of the people, acres, or acre-feet each represents, and a location already at either end of the scale cannot move further along it. Locations are modeled nodes, not measured conditions at a place.

Extreme climate stress holds the single highest of a strategy’s five values in six of the 22 strategies reported here. In 16 the highest falls under a different hydroclimate. So where a strategy’s highest value falls is not settled by construction.

How to read the values

The tier scale runs from 1 to 4.99. Tier 1 is the favourable end of the scale: the tier framework names Tier 1 Optimal and Tier 4 Critical, so a smaller value sits nearer Optimal and a larger value nearer Critical. The API serves this number in a field named weighted_score; that name describes how the number is computed, and does not mean that a larger number is a more favourable result.

Two numbers describe each result. The integer tier_level is the tier label. tier_continuous is the position within the band of that label, and always falls inside it: tier_continuous is at least tier_level and less than tier_level plus one. Labels, counts and prose use tier_level; figure positions and statistics use tier_continuous.

Tier 1 is Optimal, Tier 2 is Acceptable, Tier 3 is At-risk, and Tier 4 is Critical. These are definitional category names supplied by the tier framework; what a category means for any reader, community, or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

An area value is the unweighted mean over the locations in that area. Every location counts equally, regardless of the people, acres, or acre-feet it represents.

Any difference between two values under different hydroclimates contains both a climate effect and an operations effect; the two are separable only through the decomposition, because each is defined against its own reference. A strategy is compared with current operations under the same hydroclimate. Comparing one strategy with itself across hydroclimates is a different quantity: the climate effect plus the change in the operations effect.

One threshold governs every claim and every count here. A difference smaller than 0.1 tier units is not reported as movement, and a location is not counted as differing from its comparison. A tier is a summary built so that results in nine sectors with no shared unit can be compared, and 0.1 tier units is the finest reading it carries; a smaller difference is left unstated rather than described.

The five hydroclimates are a purposive sample of climate stress levels. They are not a sequence, a trend, or a forecast, they carry no probabilities, and no mean is taken over them.

A run ID identifies one modeling run of one strategy under one hydroclimate. It is an internal identifier, used for citation and for tracing a value back to the data platform, not a name for the strategy; the public identity of a strategy is its descriptive name.

Tier values are reported to one decimal place throughout this brief — in the prose, in the figure and in the table. The tier scale does not carry meaning finer than that. Each effect shown is the difference between the floored values, except that an effect smaller than the 0.1 tier units this brief reports movement at is shown as 0.0, so a row containing one does not add up. Values are floored rather than rounded, so a printed value stays inside the band its own label names; full-precision values are in the machine-readable sidecar this brief is built from.
