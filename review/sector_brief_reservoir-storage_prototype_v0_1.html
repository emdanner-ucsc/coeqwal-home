Reservoir storage: modeled tier results span Tier 1 (optimal) to Tier 3 (at-risk) across 23 management strategies and five hydroclimates

A by-sector results brief — one area of the water system, every strategy that has a brief. Prototype v0_1, 2026-07-27. Internal, for the COEQWAL team.

Across 23 management strategies and five modeled hydroclimates, modeled tier values for reservoir storage span Tier 1 (optimal) to Tier 3 (at-risk), from 1.9 to 3.9. Reservoir storage today — current operations under the historical hydroclimate — sits at 2.5, in Tier 2 (acceptable). Each strategy is compared to current operations under the same hydroclimate.

What this is

This brief reports modeled tier results for Reservoir storage in CalSim 3 simulations, for every management strategy in the COEQWAL scenario library that has a results brief, under each of the five modeled hydroclimates. It reports one sector across many strategies; the existing results briefs report one strategy across all nine sectors. The numbers are the same numbers.

Selection rule, stated in full. All 23 strategies with a v0_3 results brief are reported, in every one of the 5 hydroclimates, giving 115 modeled results. Nothing is chosen for being large, small, or interesting, and no result is visually emphasised over another. Strategy 4.2, prioritizing functional delivery levels to community water systems, is absent: its run family is not present in the locked 2026-07-02 snapshot, so it has no brief and is not imputed here. Rows are ordered with the reference strategy first and the remainder alphabetically.

How to read the values

Tier values are reported to one decimal place throughout this brief — in the prose, in the figure and in the table. The tier scale does not carry meaning finer than that, and printing three decimals would imply a precision the modeling does not support. Each contribution shown is the difference between the rounded values, so the terms add exactly as printed; every one is within 0.1 of the modeled term. Full-precision values are in the machine-readable sidecars this brief is built from. One consequence is worth stating: a tier band is the floor of the modeled value, not of the rounded display, so a value just below a boundary prints as that boundary and keeps the lower band. In this brief that is Groundwater pumping limits and reduced crop acreage in the Central Valley under Moderate climate stress, printed as 2.0 (modeled 1.989, Tier 1).

Each cell carries a continuous tier value and the tier band it falls in. The band is the floor of the modeled value, so 2.5 sits in Tier 2. The four bands are definitional categories supplied by the tier framework: Tier 1 Optimal, Tier 2 Acceptable, Tier 3 At-risk, Tier 4 Critical. The framework supplies the names; what a band means for any reader, community or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

The same 115 results as numbers

Each cell reads top to bottom: the hydroclimate contribution, the strategy contribution, then the resulting value with its tier band. The two contributions add to the difference between that scenario and reservoir storage today under current operations and historical hydroclimate, which sits at 2.5. The hydroclimate contribution is constant down each column by construction — it is also printed once in the column header, where its being the same in every row is the point — but it is repeated in each cell so a row can be read across without looking back up. The coloured chip at the left of each value is its tier band — green Tier 1, blue Tier 2, orange Tier 3, red Tier 4, the same four bands keyed above and shaded in the figure. No band is given more visual weight than another. A shaded cell is one where the two contributions run opposite ways, so the difference from today is smaller than either term alone: 46 of the 115. Values are to one decimal place. Each hydroclimate column and each strategy row carries its own address for citation.

Where the difference from today comes from

The difference between any cell and Reservoir storage today separates into two parts that are independent by construction: the part contributed by the hydroclimate, and the part contributed by the management strategy. They are the two arrow lengths in Figure 1 and the two figures carried by the table above — the hydroclimate contribution in each column header, the strategy contribution in each cell.

The hydroclimate contribution is measured on current operations under that hydroclimate, so it does not depend on which strategy is being read and is common to every strategy in a column. That is why it is drawn once in the figure and called out once in the column header, though it is repeated in each cell so that a row reads on its own.

The strategy contribution is what differs between strategies. It is the distance along the tier axis from current operations under the same hydroclimate — the length of a blue arrow in Figure 1 — and it is measured against the same hydroclimate, never against the historical baseline.

Where the two run opposite ways, the difference from today is smaller than either term alone; that is the case in 46 of the 115 results reported here, and those cells are shaded in the table.

What this doesn't say

This brief reports modeled outcomes from a specific ensemble. It does not predict what will happen, evaluate whether outcomes are acceptable, or recommend any course of action. The tier framework provides definitional categories; the meaning of those categories for any individual reader, community, or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

Two limits specific to this brief. The strategies reported are those the project has simulated and written briefs for; they are a sample of what could be modeled, not a census of what is possible, and the absence of a combination from this table is not evidence about that combination. And a single sector read alone says nothing about the others — a strategy's contribution in this sector may differ in sign and size from its contribution elsewhere, which is what the nine-sector results briefs are for.
