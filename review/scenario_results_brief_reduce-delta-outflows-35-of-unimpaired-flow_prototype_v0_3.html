What this is

This is a results brief: it reports the modeled tier results, and nothing else, for the reduce Delta outflows (35% of unimpaired flow) strategy — one of 24 management strategies in the COEQWAL scenario library — simulated once under each of five hydroclimates and read as a set. A scenario is a strategy paired with one hydroclimate; the five hydroclimates are a sample of stress levels, not a forecast of which will occur. All nine areas of the water system are reported under every hydroclimate. For what this strategy changes about operations, see the reduce Delta outflows (35% of unimpaired flow) strategy brief.

Today, and the two contributions

Today is current operations under the historical hydroclimate. It is the one reference this brief asks a reader to learn, and it is the same value in every column, because it does not depend on which hydroclimate a scenario pairs with.

Any difference between a scenario and today comes from two places, and they add exactly:

The hydroclimate contribution is current operations under that hydroclimate minus current operations today — the same operations, a different hydroclimate. The strategy contribution is this strategy minus current operations under that same hydroclimate — the only comparison that holds the hydroclimate fixed, and therefore the only one that isolates what the strategy does. Together they equal the difference from today.

Tier results are reported as a value on a continuous axis from Tier 1 to Tier 4.99, with the four tier bands as shaded zones; the whole number below any value is its band. A whole number sits on the leading edge of its band — a value of 4 marks the start of the Tier 4 zone, not the interior of Tier 3 — so a whole-number mark lands on a zone boundary and would otherwise misread by one band. The tier scale is ordered by definition, Tier 1 through Tier 4; a band is a category and a position on the axis is a modeled magnitude, neither is a judgment.

Five of the areas — Community water systems, Agriculture, Environmental flows, Reservoir storage, Groundwater storage — are computed from many measurement points, so their values carry detail within a band and are printed to two decimals. The other four — Bay-Delta estuary ecology, Winter-run Chinook salmon, Freshwater for Delta exports, Freshwater for in-Delta uses — carry a single whole-number tier value: on the figure they are drawn as square marks on the tier axis, in the table they are printed as whole numbers, and they carry no detail within a band.

How many places a strategy reaches

A strategy changes results only where its rules apply, and an area’s value is the unweighted mean of its locations. This strategy changes results at 28–40 of the 74 Community water systems locations, 62–73 of the 132 Agriculture locations, as few as 8 and as many as all 17 Environmental flows locations, 6–7 of the 8 Reservoir storage locations and 37–40 of the 42 Groundwater storage locations, depending on the hydroclimate — 156 to 175 of the 273 locations reported here. An area where few locations change therefore shows a small area-level difference even when the locations that do change move by a full tier or more. The remaining four areas (Bay-Delta estuary ecology, Winter-run Chinook salmon, Freshwater for Delta exports, Freshwater for in-Delta uses) are served as a single area-level value with no separate locations.

What this doesn’t say

This brief reports modeled outcomes from a specific ensemble. It does not predict what will happen, evaluate whether outcomes are acceptable, or recommend any course of action. The tier framework provides definitional categories; the meaning of those categories for any individual reader, community, or decision is not specified by COEQWAL.

In particular, for this brief: the five hydroclimates are a sample of stress levels, carry no probabilities, and are not a timeline, so no average over them is reported here. Splitting a difference into two contributions is arithmetic on modeled values, not a causal claim about the water system, and the two are separable only because each is defined against its own reference. A step or a spread on the tier axis is a modeled magnitude, not a benefit or a cost. An area’s value is the unweighted mean of its locations, so locations count equally regardless of the people, acres, or acre-feet each represents, and a location already at either end of the scale cannot move further along it. Locations are modeled nodes, not measured conditions at a place.

Under this strategy, extreme climate stress holds the single highest of an area’s five values in six of the nine areas. In three areas (Bay-Delta estuary ecology, Winter-run Chinook salmon, Freshwater for Delta exports) the highest value is shared by more than one hydroclimate.
